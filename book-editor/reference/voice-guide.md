# VOICE GUIDE: ALLIE / CHOOSE ME SERIES

This is the law for every line-level suggestion. The voice is not being developed. It already exists. Editing means protecting it, not polishing it.

## THE SIGNATURE STRUCTURE

Allie's voice has a five-beat pattern. When a passage feels flat, check it against this, not against literary prose standards:
1. State the absurd thing plainly ("Two guys. Same house. Same night. Roommates.")
2. Make a dark joke ("If there was a Whore Olympics, I'd just medaled.")
3. Try to find the humor ("I tried to make a joke about it in my head.")
4. Fail to find it ("But I couldn't find the punchline.")
5. Land the real hit ("And that scared me more than the shame.")

The bravado carries the piece. The last line lands the truth.

## HARD VOICE RULES

- No em dashes. No en dashes. Ever.
- Short punchy paragraphs. One idea per paragraph.
- Dark humor is a voice signature. Protect it. Never editorialize it, explain it, or soften it. She cracked jokes at 10cm with no epidural. This is wiring.
- Story first. Always. No thesis statements inside chapters.
- Trust the scene. Never explain what the reader should feel.
- Every sentence earns the next one.
- Ellipses are a pacing tool, not an error.
- Specific scene-grounded details over abstractions. The Subway wrapper, the bass from the Pike's speakers, the marker. Concrete beats conceptual every time.
- Direct, imperative closes where they occur. Do not round them off.

## WHAT THIS VOICE IS NOT

- Not literary. Do not measure against prose stylists. It is visceral, confessional, and true rather than beautiful. If a suggestion makes a line more elegant and less bleeding, kill the suggestion.
- Not healed. She writes from the middle, with more questions than answers. Any line that sounds like a mountaintop retrospective in Books 1-5 is wrong.
- Not preachy. God is the trojan horse. He is woven through the events (protection, grace, timing) without being announced. Faith content shows; it never tells. The reader discovers Him in the narrative, not the narration.

## CHAPTER MECHANICS

- Every chapter hooks in the first two lines. No slow warmups.
- Vary opening patterns; never the same opening style twice in a row.
- Time and place clear within the first paragraph. On major time jumps, the reader always knows when they are.
- One gut-punch per chapter maximum, placed at chapter end or before a POV switch. Simple language, maximum impact.
- Aftermath emotions must vary across the fuck-up chapters. The events differ; the endings must too. The progression is denial, then cracks, then numbness, then surrender. Check each ending against its assigned emotional register in the Book 1 endings doc.

## POV VOICES

Each POV must sound like that person, not like Allie writing them.
- **Jon:** his silence has to hurt from inside his own head. Pride, fear, self-protection, assumptions never voiced.
- **Beca (Jade in manuscript):** her own rhythm. She sees what Allie can't; her chapters carry tension from watching it unfold.
- **Phylecia:** her own rhythm, real name by her request, co-narrator across all six books.
- **Tyler (Books 4-6):** per Tyler_Voice_Guide_Complete. Short sentences. Casual. Surface-level. Pulls back from depth immediately. Passive language about his own inaction ("things fell off," never "I chose not to"). Tells the truth about himself without realizing what he's revealing. The reader sees what he can't. Never make Tyler self-aware.
- **Eliah:** distinct voice, redemption arc parallel to Allie's that lands differently.

## EXPLICIT CONTENT

Every explicit scene must reveal character or advance story. Emotional truth over physical detail. Honest but dignified. Never sanitize, never sensationalize. If a scene does neither job, FLAG it; do not trim it unilaterally.
